Buying a Used Boat in BC: Licence Transfer, Bill of Sale & the 12% PST

The handshake is the easy part. What catches BC buyers out is the paperwork that follows a private boat sale — a federal licence transfer with a deadline most people have wrong, a provincial tax bill nobody mentions at the dock, and a trailer that gets taxed on a completely different basis than the boat sitting on it. Here is the sequence, in the order you should do it.

Step 1: Get a bill of sale that covers the right things

There is no BC-government bill of sale form for boats. The content standard that matters is Transport Canada's, because your bill of sale is the proof of ownership you'll use for the licence transfer — and for the tax step below.

Transport Canada requires a proof-of-ownership document to include:

  • The seller's name, address and signature
  • Your name and signature, as the new owner
  • A description of the craft (make, model, colour)
  • The hull serial number (HIN), if available
  • The Pleasure Craft Licence number, if the boat is already licensed

On top of that core, add the items Transport Canada doesn't list but that protect you in practice: the date of sale, the purchase price, the outboard's serial number, the trailer's VIN, and a statement from the seller that the boat is sold free of liens. The date matters because two clocks start at the ownership change (see below). The price matters because BC calculates your PST on it. The serial numbers matter because they're what a lien search runs on — a boat package is really three searches: hull, motor and trailer.

Step 2: Transfer the Pleasure Craft Licence within 30 days

If the boat already has a Canadian Pleasure Craft Licence (PCL), the number stays with the boat for its entire life. The seller should not cancel it — your job is to transfer it into your name.

The deadline is 30 days from the ownership change, and you must transfer before operating the boat. You may still see 90 days quoted on older pages and registry-service sites; that was the old rule, and it changed at the end of 2025. Work to 30.

To transfer, you'll need ID for all owners, your proof of ownership (the bill of sale from Step 1), and a current colour photo of the boat's full side view. The transfer fee is $24. Apply online and Transport Canada emails you a temporary licence, valid 30 days, so you can use the boat right away; the permanent licence carries the same number. New, transferred and renewed licences are now valid for five years. Keep the licence document on board — that's a requirement, not a suggestion — and know that operating without a valid licence can draw a $250 fine.

One thing a PCL is not: proof of ownership. Transport Canada says so in plain language — like a car's licence plate, it's identification. The document that actually evidences your purchase is your bill of sale, so file the original somewhere safe.

Step 3: Pay the BC PST — 12% on a private sale

This is the step that surprises people. Buy a boat privately in BC and you owe 12% PST on the purchase price, including accessories. Gear that comes with the boat — motors, fishing gear, water-sport equipment — is taxed at the same rate as the boat, even if it's listed separately on your bill of sale.

Nobody collects this from you at the point of sale, because in a private sale there's no collector. You self-assess: if you don't have a PST number (most buyers don't), you file a Casual Remittance Return (FIN 405) and pay on or before the last day of the month following the month you obtained the boat.

Don't treat that deadline casually. The province has said it routinely matches Transport Canada's ownership-change records against PST payments — so the licence transfer in Step 2 is exactly what can trigger a letter asking you to confirm you paid. The two steps are connected whether you like it or not.

Buying from a dealer instead? A purchase from a GST registrant carries 7% PST, collected by the dealer — one of the quieter differences between the private and dealer routes.

Step 4: Deal with the trailer separately

The trailer is not taxed like the boat. Trailers fall under BC's vehicle PST rules, not the boat rules in the same bulletin. If PST wasn't paid on the trailer at purchase, ICBC will charge it when you register the trailer. Keep your receipt and the ICBC Transfer/Tax Form (APV9T) as your proof of payment.

Practical upshot: when you're writing up the deal, it helps to show the boat, motor and trailer as separate line items with their own values. It makes the tax treatment of each piece cleaner, and it matches how the searches and registrations actually work.

Bought the boat outside BC?

The tax follows the boat into the province:

  • Private sale elsewhere in Canada, brought into BC: 12% on the depreciated purchase price. The depreciated value can't fall below 50% of the purchase price; depreciation runs at 15% per year plus 1.25% per 30 days for part years.
  • Dealer purchase elsewhere in Canada: 7% on the depreciated purchase price.
  • Brought in from outside Canada: 7% on the taxable value. Customs may collect at the border; if they don't, you self-assess the same way as above.
  • A gift: generally 12% on fair market value, with 7% applying in specific cases.

Licensed or registered — know which one your boat is

Most BC pleasure craft are licensed (the PCL system above). Some are registered in the Canadian Register of Vessels, which is a different thing entirely: Transport Canada describes the register as a title system, and it's the only place a marine mortgage can be recorded. Registration is voluntary for a pleasure craft unless a trigger applies — the common ones are a lender requiring a registered mortgage, or wanting a reserved name for the boat. If you're buying a registered vessel, the transfer process is different from everything on this page, and it's worth getting the details before money moves.

The short version

Bill of sale with the right contents on day one. Licence transferred within 30 days. FIN 405 filed and 12% PST paid by the end of the following month. Trailer sorted at ICBC. Do those four things and the paperwork side of a BC private purchase is genuinely finished.
